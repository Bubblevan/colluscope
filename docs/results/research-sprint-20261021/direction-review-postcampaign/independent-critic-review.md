## Recommendation

The new reanalysis moves **B — the private-target-reward × chat-stage package interaction** to the top as the most promising prospective direction. It does not establish a semantic message effect, collusion, or population-level behavior.

Across 75 completed episodes in 15 matched groups, the target-execution difference-in-differences was +1 in 9 groups, zero in 6, and negative in none; mean regret interaction was +9. The target and regret statistics arise from the same groups and are not independent confirmations. The pattern is heterogeneous by payoff family: target execution in the combined condition was 1/5, 5/5, and 3/5 across families 1–3. It was present at every combined-condition order position, but the order-position cell sizes are uneven; the data do not include a prespecified order-sensitivity analysis.

The combination includes both a private target bonus and an additional message stage/prompt. Both agents know the same rewarded target, and their message actions are generated simultaneously before either can receive the other's message. The observed contrast is therefore about the assigned package in this fixed corpus. It cannot identify whether proposal semantics, stage salience, or another part of the package caused the ballots.

## Candidate ranking

1. **B — private reward × chat-stage package:** highest potential scientific value from the new real-agent evidence; moderate identifiability for the finite assigned package contrast, low for message semantics or intent. A preregistered replication is needed before making it confirmatory.
2. **A — B5 response sensitivity/repeatability:** strongest audit evidence, but the 232-request campaign remains scientifically inconclusive. The 48 exact-request comparisons establish finite raw-response disagreement for these observations, not its cause. Broad information-access and nondeterminism claims overlap prior work.
3. **C — evidence sufficiency/identifiability:** good fit to Track 2.3, but the central matched-world result is a finite scripted construction. It is not a general impossibility result or a prevalence estimate.

The Sprint page's Track 2.2 question is a monitor-view comparison; the M1 result is an Agent behavior result, not a monitor-accuracy comparison. Track 2.3 is the more direct fit for evidence scope. The B5 findings should remain a Track 2.2 measurement caveat. None of these results tests steganographic communication.

## Strongest rivals and counterexamples

- **Shared-target salience/parallel choice:** A and B know the same rewarded target in advance. The 9 target-concordant episodes do not by themselves show that one Agent's message changed another's choice.
- **Extra-stage/prompt effect:** a chat-stage prompt can change behavior even if the message's allocation meaning does not. A matched stage with neutral or undelivered content is needed to separate this from message semantics.
- **Family dependence:** the combined-condition result is 1/5 in family 1, 5/5 in family 2, and 3/5 in family 3. The nine positive groups do not support a uniform effect across payoff structures.
- **Order and temporal effects:** the combined condition's positive cases occur at positions 0–4, which weakens a simple position-only explanation, but assignment counts across condition × position are uneven and no sensitivity test was preregistered.

The strongest counterexample to interpreting harmful outcomes as coordination is M1-100 episode 013: positive regret occurred under normal cooperation without private communication or target execution. M3 also found the same 3/8 changed ballots under Neutral and No-delivery, while Masked changed 0/8; those contrasts do not isolate message semantics.

## Next action

Preregister a confirmatory replication of the package interaction using new matched scenario/seed groups before making it a headline. A 15-group, five-condition extension would assign 75 episodes: 225 ballot requests plus 60 chat-message requests, for 285 logical requests. Counterbalance order, preserve failures without replacement, and freeze new payoff matrices before any response. This is a planning estimate only; the existing 232-request DeepSeek authorization does not cover Agent generation, a new model, or a new experiment. Fresh scope-specific approval is required.

The present M1 finding should be described as post-hoc and finite. A positive replication would support only the fixed-model treatment-package contrast; a null or family-specific replication would weaken a general package claim. Neither outcome alone identifies collusion or intent.
